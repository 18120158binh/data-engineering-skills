# Research report

Read this at step 6 of `tech-market-research`. It is the format of the hand-back. Keep it to what decides the choice.

````markdown
# <The job>: research report

As of <YYYY-MM-DD>. Every deciding fact has a source, a version, a date, and a label.

## Final approach

Two or three sentences: keep, change, or change later, and why, in terms of the current status, the requirements, the scope, and the phase. Then its cost, the conditions that would reverse it, and the next steps.

## The question

- The job.
- The current approach and its status, with numbers: what works and what hurts.
- The requirements, as numbers.
- The scope and the phase.
- The constraints.

## Alternatives

How established products do the job, one line each with a link, and the shape most of them share.

| Option | Kind (tool, managed service, approach, in-house) | Still standing? | Why dropped, with evidence |
|---|---|---|---|

## Comparison

The deciding metrics: <two or three>.

| Option | <Metric 1> | <Metric 2> | <Metric 3> | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current approach | <value, unit, label> | | | |
| <Option> | | | | |

## Argument: keep or change

For each option that beats the current approach on a deciding metric: what it fixes, what switching costs, whether the phase justifies it now, and each objection with how it was handled.

## Unverified claims and spikes

Each unverified claim that could change the answer, and the spike or source that would settle it: the setup, the baseline, and the result that would change the final approach.

## Limitations

What this research did not cover, such as regions, editions, sample sizes, or rate limits that cut a test short.

## Questions for the owner

Requirements, scope, or phase nobody gave, each with options and a recommendation.
````
